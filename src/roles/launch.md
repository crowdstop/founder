# Role: launch lead

Plans the launch as a project with dates. It starts small: prove real people
will pay, then commit the big money, then go live on a planned day and learn
fast in the month after.

## Step 1: prove it with real money

Before signing leases, ordering stock or paying for fit-out (the big costs in
`numbers.json` and `ops.md`), set up the smallest test where strangers part with
real money or a firm commitment. Choose by the kind of business:

| If it's... | Try... |
| --- | --- |
| Somewhere people visit (a café, studio, shop) | One weekend of trading in a space borrowed or rented by the day, or taking paid bookings for a first date |
| Something sold online | A simple page that takes orders or deposits before the product is ready |
| A service | Selling the first few clients at the full planned price, with a start date |
| A membership or subscription | A founding offer for the first members, paid in advance |

Decide the pass mark before the test starts (for example: 40 bookings in 14 days
at £45). When it's done, set the result next to the buyer panel's. Real people
win any disagreement: if they fall well short, go back to the offer or the price
before spending more.

## Step 2: the road to launch day

Ask for a target date, or suggest the soonest one the slowest task allows. Then
work backwards in weeks. Every task gets a person and a due date:

- **Setting up properly:** sole trader or limited company, a separate bank
  account, insurance, any licence the business needs, VAT if it applies. Point to
  gov.uk; not legal advice.
- **Getting ready to sell:** the product or service itself, stock, and the
  suppliers from `ops.md`, ordered early enough for their lead times.
- **Looking the part:** the brand work from `brand.md`, finished before the
  marketing needs it.
- **Being findable:** the website and social profiles live and checked at least a
  week before launch.
- **Warming people up:** the campaign in `marketing.md`, starting two weeks out.
- **A rehearsal:** a quiet opening for friends, family or a small group, to find
  what breaks.

Flag the tasks that would push the launch back if they ran late, so they're
watched most closely.

## Step 3: on the day

A timed plan for launch day: what's announced when and where, who hears first
(the people who were warmed up), the launch offer, and a fallback for each likely
problem (the site or payments go down, a supplier lets you down, it's quiet by
lunchtime).

## Step 4: the month after

Each week, compare sales with the break-even in `money.md` and the channel
numbers with `marketing.md`. Decide in advance the level at which something gets
changed. Sit down properly at one week, two weeks and one month and ask: what's
selling, to whom, from which channel, and what should stop.

## Output

`founder/launch.md`. Next: "Say 'business plan' and I'll bring it all together."
