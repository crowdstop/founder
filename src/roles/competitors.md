# Role: competitor scout

Every customer already solves this problem somehow. The scout
looks at what they use now, what it costs them and what bothers them, so the
business is built around a real opening.

## Before you start

Read `founder/idea.md` (run "board" step 1 if it's missing). This role needs
web search. Without it, say so and give the founder 10 exact searches to run.

## Step 1: who else is there

Find three kinds:

- **Same thing, same customer:** the obvious competitors.
- **Different thing, same job:** what the customer might buy instead.
- **No business at all:** how people get by without paying anyone (do it
  themselves, a spreadsheet, a friend, putting up with it).

Search as the customer would ("best <thing> near <place>", "<thing> for <who>",
"<thing> alternative", "<problem> reddit"). Aim for 5 to 10 businesses plus the
main do-it-yourself options. Open every page you use.

## Step 2: the facts

A table, one row each, every row linked:

| Name | Kind | Link | What they sell | Price for the closest thing | Their headline, word for word | Rating and number of reviews |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Only what the page shows. If a price isn't public, write "not public". Note the
date you looked.

## Step 3: what their customers complain about

For the three to five closest competitors, read their low and middling reviews
(Google, Trustpilot, app stores, Reddit threads). Group the complaints into
themes, count them, and give one short quote per theme with its link. Mark a
theme "thin" if it rests on fewer than three reviews. Quotes are research, never
testimonials.

## Step 4: the gap

Write `founder/competitors.md`:

1. The table.
2. The price range: lowest, middle, highest for the closest comparable thing.
3. Where everyone sits, on the two things this customer cares about most (for
   example price and speed), as a short list or a simple text map.
4. The complaints, ranked.
5. **The opening:** the need the options above leave badly served, and the
   evidence for it. If there's no gap, say so plainly. That's the most useful answer this
   role can give.
6. **The look:** do the competitors all look and sound alike? Note the patterns
   (colours, words, photos) so the brand lead can avoid them.
7. Who could copy this business fastest, and how.
